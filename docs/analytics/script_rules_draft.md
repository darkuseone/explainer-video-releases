# Evidence-led scripts for short explainers

Use when writing or revising a short educational video, or applying a new weekly analytics export. This is a draft body, not an installed skill. Channel-specific voices, accounts, destinations and production specs belong in the caller's brief.

Current-snapshot evidence is kept separately in `script_rule_evidence.md`; do not turn those figures into universal thresholds.

## 1. Establish what is known

Load the latest valid analytics snapshot, previous experiment log and source-supported topic brief. Separate measured findings from hypotheses and user requirements. If exports are unavailable, say so and write from the brief; never claim the script is data-optimized from unseen files. Check factual premises before brainstorming hooks. Never turn a rumor into an announcement or generated imagery into evidence. Preserve scientific uncertainty in plain language.

## 2. Write three genuinely different openings

The FIRST spoken sentence must simultaneously identify the subject/situation and create a concrete, truthful reason to continue. No greeting, unnamed 'this', generic suspense or context-free fragments. Pair it with an immediately relevant visual, not unrelated spectacle. Choose the strongest understandable opening, not necessarily a question every time.

Examples, illustrative rather than reusable catchphrases:
- «Чеснок меняет запах дыхания на несколько часов, потому что часть его веществ выходит через лёгкие.»
- «Если время появилось вместе со Вселенной, что вообще значит “до Большого взрыва”?»
- «OLED включает каждый пиксель отдельно, поэтому чёрный участок экрана может вообще не светиться.»

Each example still requires topic-specific fact-checking and visual planning. Do not recycle it mechanically. Reject a hook that can be pasted unchanged into three unrelated videos.

## 3. Build a causal story, not a fragment chain

Write full spoken sentences. Every sentence answers the previous sentence's question, explains its cause, adds evidence, or states a consequence. One central question, one clear takeaway. A cold viewer must understand the video without title, description, previous episode or specialist knowledge.

Bad: «OLED. Чёрный. Дорого. Но есть нюанс.»
Better: «В OLED каждый пиксель светится сам. Поэтому чёрный участок может полностью выключиться. Это делает тёмные сцены контрастнее, но само по себе не гарантирует точные цвета.»

Complete sentences may be brief. Do not enforce a tiny word limit or speed up narration to fit excessive facts. Leave time to process the key idea. Cut redundant information, not the grammatical connection that makes it understandable.

## 4. Explain terms immediately

Define necessary jargon in the same line or with a readable synchronized visual that needs no prior knowledge. Prefer plain words when the term adds no value.
Example: «Ген — это участок ДНК с инструкцией, по которой клетка создаёт функциональный продукт, например белок.» Simplify further for the specific claim if needed, without converting 'gene' into 'one trait'.

Do not add slang solely to sound youthful. If slang is essential, explain what it means right there. Verify pronunciation, caption fit and enough reading time.

## 5. Earn attention throughout

Deliver an early piece of the answer, then develop the mechanism/evidence and a satisfying payoff. Do not withhold the whole answer behind filler. Use only the story turns the topic warrants, not mandatory mini-loops at fixed timestamps. A visual change should clarify a change in thought. No forced beat every two seconds.

Plan one fresh analogy only when it reduces cognitive effort; explicitly stop it where it becomes misleading. Never repeat previously praised metaphors by default. Read the script aloud with eyes closed: if only the animation makes its logic work, repair the narration.

## 6. Add authorial perspective, sometimes humor

Humor or gentle sarcasm is optional and topic-dependent, never a quota. Aim at a situation, misconception or marketing excess, not a vulnerable person or the viewer's intelligence. The joke must be understandable and must not become a false scientific claim.
Example: «Красивое название технологии ещё не делает экран хорошим. Маркетинг, к сожалению, пиксели не калибрует.»

Ban stock fake-twist transitions such as «а вот поворот». Replace them with the actual causal statement, not a synonym. Vary evidence, narrative approach, imagery and conclusion materially; palette swaps and topic swaps alone are not originality.

## 7. Give one relevant invitation, only if earned

After the payoff, optionally ask one answerable question connected to the explanation, or give a specific reason to subscribe. Do not stack requests for like/comment/share/subscribe. A natural thematic loop must not leave an incomplete sentence or hide the answer to force a replay.

Comment example: «Какой миф о чесноке разобрать следующим — про простуду или про давление?» Do not imply either claim is true or solicit personal medical histories.
Subscription example: «Здесь мы проверяем громкие научные обещания по источникам.» Use only if this accurately describes the channel. These are hypotheses to test, not established conversion tricks.

No paid/fake engagement, sub-for-sub, rewards or coercion. Never provoke harmful misinformation simply to generate corrections in comments.

## 8. Originality and policy gate

Make original educational or entertainment value obvious: a distinct question, researched explanation, meaningful commentary and topic-specific visual evidence. Reuse production utilities, not interchangeable story substance. Same intro/outro alone is not prohibited; AI alone is not prohibited. Review channel-level repetition as well as this video's originality. Reused-content review and copyright clearance are separate. Label synthetic scenes and disclose realistic alterations when policy requires it.

## 9. Before recording

- First-line test: a new viewer can name the subject and why it matters.
- Chain test: annotate how each complete sentence follows the previous one; repair unexplained jumps.
- Terms test: every necessary term is defined immediately in speech or a readable visual.
- Truth test: each factual claim has a credible source; uncertainty is explicit.
- Voice test: natural read at the approved pace, no chopped endings to hit runtime.
- Promise test: the ending pays off the actual hook; no misleading reveal.
- Novelty test: compare recent scripts; replace stock phrasing, recycled analogies and identical causal structure.
- CTA test: optional, one relevant invitation, no interruption of the explanation.
- Measurement test: record exact hook, expected primary metric, baseline scope, guardrails and one main variable changed.

## 10. Weekly feedback loop

1. Audit and archive originals; identify actual report types, periods, filters, units, IDs and missing metrics.
2. Append one canonical video row per snapshot, idempotently. Preserve raw exports and source references; never overwrite prior observations silently.
3. Compare equal-age, similar-length/source cohorts. Distinguish starts, engaged views, stayed-to-watch, AVD/APV, satisfaction and subscriber conversion. Show raw denominators; do not manufacture missing rates.
4. Compare best/worst scripts only after matching the actual published versions. Code hook, clarity, progression, topic, runtime and CTA. Treat weekday/topic associations as confounded, not causal.
5. Inspect timed retention with the transcript. Without curves, do not assign a drop to a phrase. Separate Observed, Hypothesis and Next Test.
6. Pre-register one main change for a comparable set of upcoming videos. Judge primarily by the target metric, with AVD and subscriber conversion as guardrails. A later upload is not a randomized A/B test.
7. Retain only repeated, reasonably supported lessons as general writing rules. Keep explicit user requirements regardless of whether a single test wins. Publish no private analytics to an external repository without the owner's explicit permission.

YouTube's Shorts discovery guide names chose-to-view, AVD, APV, likes and post-watch surveys; it does not publish an exact viral cutoff.
